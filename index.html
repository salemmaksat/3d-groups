Background

What SO(3) is

SO(3) is the set of all rotations of 3D space about a fixed point. As matrices, it is every 3×3 matrix R with RᵀR = I and det R = 1. Doing one rotation after another gives another rotation, so the set is a group.

It takes three numbers to pin down a rotation: two for the axis and one for the angle. So SO(3) is also a three-dimensional space you can move around in.

Why it does not fit in 3D

Take the ball model. To make it exact, you would glue every point of the surface to the point opposite it. The result has no surface at all. It is a closed 3D space, called real projective space ℝP³.

A closed 3D space can't sit inside ordinary 3D space, just as a sphere can't lie flat on a table. ℝP³ needs five dimensions to sit in without gluing. A printed Klein bottle cheats by letting its surface pass through itself. A solid can't pass through itself, so every model here marks the gluing with color, shape or position instead.

How to read the models

  • The yellow dot is the rotation set by the sliders.
  • The small window shows an object turned by that rotation. The gray arrow is the rotation axis.
  • In the ball, shell, slice and quaternion models, the center is “no rotation”.
  • Same color or same shape means same rotation.
  • Spin past 180° and the dot jumps, while the object keeps turning smoothly. That jump is the gluing.
  • The spin slider goes to 720°. In SO(3), 360° and 720° look the same. In SU(2) they don’t.
